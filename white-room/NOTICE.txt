## The world behind the blue door

The underwater world behind the blue door is the scene of Abyssal Garden, brought over from its source.
Everything in `src/abyss/` is that scene's code, adapted to stand behind a doorway:

- `src/abyss/layout.js`: the scene's layout tables, viewpoints and camera path.
- `src/abyss/world.js`: its world generator.
- `src/abyss/meshes.js`: its procedural meshes (rocks, sponges, coral, the whale fluke, the wreck, the manta, the fish).
- `src/abyss/wgsl.js`: its shaders (terrain density, caustics, materials, kelp, animals, water surface, particles, light shafts).
- `src/abyss/renderer.js`: its draw calls, draw distances and levels of detail.
- `src/abyss/build.js`: its build steps and their order.
- `src/audio.js`: its soundscape (rumble, wash, rush, drone, bubbles, chimes, whale calls).
- `src/player.js`: its swimming (speeds, how the body takes them up, sliding along rock).
- `src/wgsl/post.js`: its grade, applied where the underwater world shows.

What White Room adds to that world is the blue door: the door and the plinth it stands on, the pipelines that draw the world through the doorway, the doorway's light in the water, and the water laid over the room seen from it.
Nothing is generated where the door stands; the rest of the world comes out as it does in Abyssal Garden.

## The jungle and the room

These parts of the renderer for the room and the jungle are derived from Abyssal Garden, and are marked in the source files that contain them:

- `src/math.js`: seeded random streams, value noise, quaternion helpers.
- `src/geometry.js`: surface nets, the trilinear sampler, the mesh builder core, the icosphere.
- `src/world/terrain.js` and `src/wgsl/density.js`: the density pipeline and the rock formulas.
- `src/wgsl/textures.js`: the detail texture and the bloom chain.
- `src/wgsl/solid.js`: triplanar detail and layered rock.
- `src/wgsl/leaf.js`: the instanced strip that the vertex shader bends and sways.
- `src/wgsl/post.js`: the depth-aware blur and upsample, the tone mapping.
- `src/renderer.js`: the frame structure, cell culling, GPU timing and dynamic resolution.

## The lighthouse behind the pink door

The lighthouse's surfaces are photographed texture sets and its sound is recorded, all published by their authors under CC0 1.0 (public domain dedication).
They are kept as files beside the page, in `assets/lighthouse/`.

| Use | Source | Credits |
|---|---|---|
| 18 texture sets: concrete, plaster, wood, stone, tiles, rock, metals, fabric, leather, carpet, brick | [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license) and [ambientCG](https://ambientcg.com/) | `assets/lighthouse/tex/CREDITS.md` names each asset, its authors and its link |
| 47 sound files: storm and calm sea, wind, rain, thunder, wave crashes, gulls, fire, clock, generator, pump, footsteps, switch, horn | [Freesound](https://freesound.org/) and [BigSoundBank](https://bigsoundbank.com/) | `assets/lighthouse/audio/CREDITS.md` names each recording, its author and its link |

The textures were resampled and repacked by `tools/place-textures.py`, the recordings cut and levelled by `tools/place-audio.py`; `tools/lighthouse-textures.json` and `tools/lighthouse-audio.json` list the sources and what was done to them.
The tower, its furnishing, the sea, the weather and the pictures on its walls are made by the page.

## The stars over the moon

The positions, magnitudes and colour indices of the stars brighter than magnitude 6.5 in the moon's sky are from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th Revised Edition (D. Hoffleit and W. H. Warren Jr., 1991), distributed by the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg as catalogue V/50.
`tools/make-stars.mjs` packs them into `src/moon/stars-data.js`; the catalogue itself is not part of this repository.
